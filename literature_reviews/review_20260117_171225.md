### 1. Advancing Explainability in Neural Machine Translation: Analytical Metrics for Attention and Alignment Consistency

**Authors:** Anurag Mishra
**ArXiv:** [2412.18669v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18669v1)
**Published:** 2024-12-24

**Contribution:** The paper introduces a quantitative framework for evaluating NMT explainability by defining attention entropy and alignment agreement metrics that compare model attention patterns with statistical word alignments.

**Method:** It computes attention entropy per target token, measures alignment agreement between attention distributions and external alignment references, and correlates these metrics with BLEU/METEOR scores on an English‑German WMT14 subset using a pre‑trained mT5 model.

**Key Findings:**
- Sharper (lower entropy) attention distributions tend to align better with statistical alignments but do not consistently improve translation quality.
Alignment agreement correlates mildly with higher BLEU/METEOR scores, yet high agreement alone does not guarantee superior performance.

### 2. Instruction-tuned Large Language Models for Machine Translation in the Medical Domain

**Authors:** Miguel Rios
**ArXiv:** [2408.16440v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16440v2)
**Published:** 2024-08-29

**Contribution:** The paper demonstrates that instruction‑tuning LLMs with domain‑specific medical terminology significantly improves machine translation quality in the medical domain compared to baseline LLMs.

**Method:** Baseline large language models were fine‑tuned on instruction‑formatted datasets that incorporate specialized medical dictionaries; performance was evaluated using automatic MT metrics and error analysis based on automated annotation.

**Key Findings:**
- Instruction‑tuned LLMs outperform baseline models on automatic translation quality metrics.
Error analysis shows a substantial reduction in terminology‑related translation errors after instruction tuning.

### 3. End-to-End Reinforcement Learning for Torque Based Variable Height Hopping

**Authors:** Raghav Soni, Daniel Harnack, Hannah Isermann, Sotaro Fushimi, Shivesh Kumar
**ArXiv:** [2307.16676v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16676v2)
**Published:** 2023-07-31

**Contribution:** The paper introduces an end‑to‑end reinforcement learning torque controller for variable‑height hopping that learns phase detection implicitly, eliminating manual heuristics, and demonstrates successful sim‑to‑real transfer without parameter tuning.

**Method:** A deep RL policy is trained in simulation to output joint torques for a single‑legged hopper; the network learns to detect lift‑off, flight, and touchdown phases internally. The learned controller is transferred to hardware using domain randomization and a sim‑to‑real adaptation technique.

**Key Findings:**
- The RL policy achieves variable‑height hopping with comparable or better performance than heuristic phase‑based controllers.
Transfer to the real robot succeeds without additional tuning, validating the sim‑to‑real approach.

### 4. Unified Sequence-to-Sequence Learning for Single- and Multi-Modal Visual Object Tracking

**Authors:** Xin Chen, Ben Kang, Jiawen Zhu, Dong Wang, Houwen Peng
**ArXiv:** [2304.14394v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14394v3)
**Published:** 2023-04-27

**Contribution:** The paper proposes SeqTrack and its extension SeqTrackv2, a unified sequence‑to‑sequence transformer framework that casts RGB and multi‑modal visual object tracking as autoregressive bounding‑box generation, eliminating task‑specific head networks.

**Method:** An encoder–decoder transformer architecture is used: the bidirectional encoder extracts features from template and search regions; the causal decoder predicts a sequence of bounding boxes (x,y,w,h) step by step. SeqTrackv2 adds a unified interface for auxiliary modalities and task‑prompt tokens to handle multiple tracking tasks with a single model.

**Key Findings:**
- SeqTrack achieves competitive or superior performance on RGB tracking benchmarks compared to head‑based trackers.
SeqTrackv2 outperforms state‑of‑the‑art methods across 14 challenging single‑ and multi‑modal tracking benchmarks.

### 5. Unsupervised Flow-Aligned Sequence-to-Sequence Learning for Video Restoration

**Authors:** Jing Lin, Xiaowan Hu, Yuanhao Cai, Haoqian Wang, Youliang Yan
**ArXiv:** [2205.10195v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10195v2)
**Published:** 2022-05-20

**Contribution:** The paper introduces S2SVR, an unsupervised flow‑aligned sequence‑to‑sequence framework that jointly learns optical flow and video restoration, enabling a single model to handle multiple VR tasks.

**Method:** S2SVR serializes the input video into a 1D token stream, applies a transformer encoder–decoder for long‑range temporal modeling, and aligns frames using an unsupervised optical‑flow estimator trained with a distillation loss that mitigates flow inaccuracies.

**Key Findings:**
- S2SVR outperforms state‑of‑the‑art methods on video deblurring, super‑resolution and compressed‑video enhancement benchmarks.
- The unsupervised flow estimator improves correspondence accuracy, leading to better restoration quality across tasks.

### 6. Simplicial Attention Neural Networks

**Authors:** L. Giusti, C. Battiloro, P. Di Lorenzo, S. Sardellitti, S. Barbarossa
**ArXiv:** [2203.07485v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07485v2)
**Published:** 2022-03-14

**Contribution:** The paper proposes Simplicial Attention Neural Networks (SANs), a novel attention‑based architecture that processes signals on simplicial complexes by jointly attending to upper and lower neighborhood structures across multiple simplex dimensions.

**Method:** SANs extend self‑attention to simplicial domains: each simplex (node, edge, triangle, etc.) receives a masked multi‑head attention over its incident higher‑order and adjacent lower‑order simplices, with learnable weighting of upper/lower neighborhoods. The model is trained end‑to‑end for tasks such as trajectory prediction and missing data imputation.

**Key Findings:**
- SANs outperform existing graph‑based and simplicial methods on inductive/transductive citation completion and trajectory forecasting benchmarks.
- The attention mechanism learns meaningful upper/lower neighborhood weights that adapt to the task without manual tuning.

### 7. SA-Net: Shuffle Attention for Deep Convolutional Neural Networks

**Authors:** Qing-Long Zhang Yu-Bin Yang
**ArXiv:** [2102.00240v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00240v1)
**Published:** 2021-01-30

**Contribution:** SA-Net introduces a lightweight Shuffle Attention (SA) module that efficiently fuses spatial and channel attention via grouped sub-features and shuffle units, achieving higher accuracy with significantly reduced parameters and FLOPs compared to existing methods.

**Method:** The SA module first partitions channels into multiple groups, applies a Shuffle Unit—combining spatial and channel attention within each group—then aggregates the groups and performs a channel‑shuffle operation to enable cross‑group communication. This design reduces computational overhead while preserving expressive power.

**Key Findings:**
- SA-Net outperforms state‑of‑the‑art attention modules such as SENet, CBAM, ECA‑Net, and SGE‑Net on ImageNet‑1k, achieving >1.34% Top‑1 accuracy gain with only 300 additional parameters and 2.76e-3 GFLOPs over ResNet‑50.
- On MS COCO benchmarks, SA-Net improves detection and segmentation metrics while maintaining lower model complexity than competing attention‑based backbones.

### 8. Pairwise Neural Machine Translation Evaluation

**Authors:** Francisco Guzman, Shafiq Joty, Lluis Marquez, Preslav Nakov
**ArXiv:** [1912.03135v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03135v1)
**Published:** 2019-12-05

**Contribution:** The paper introduces a neural network–based pairwise MT evaluation framework that learns compact lexical, syntactic and semantic representations of a reference and two hypotheses, achieving human‑judgment correlation comparable to state‑of‑the‑art metrics.

**Method:** It encodes each hypothesis and the reference using word and sentence embeddings, then feeds their concatenated vectors into a multi‑layer neural network that models interactions between each hypothesis–reference pair and between the two hypotheses; the network outputs which hypothesis is better in a binary classification setting.

**Key Findings:**
- The proposed method attains Pearson/Spearman correlations with human judgments that rival or exceed traditional metrics such as BLEU, METEOR and TER on the evaluated datasets.
- It demonstrates that a lightweight neural architecture can efficiently learn discriminative features without requiring extensive hand‑crafted linguistic resources.

### 9. Neural Machine Translation: A Review and Survey

**Authors:** Felix Stahlberg
**ArXiv:** [1912.02047v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02047v2)
**Published:** 2019-12-04

**Contribution:** The paper provides a comprehensive review and survey of neural machine translation (NMT), tracing its evolution from early encoder–decoder models to modern architectures such as attention mechanisms and Transformers, while highlighting key trends and resources in the field.

**Method:** Literature review and taxonomy construction; the authors analyze seminal papers, categorize NMT approaches, and summarize available open-source toolkits and datasets cited in the literature.

**Key Findings:**
- NMT has largely displaced statistical MT, leading to a rapid increase in publications and adoption of encoder–decoder architectures with attention.
- The survey identifies major architectural milestones (e.g., Bahdanau attention, Transformer) and catalogs popular open‑source NMT toolkits that have driven research progress.

### 10. On Using SpecAugment for End-to-End Speech Translation

**Authors:** Parnia Bahar, Albert Zeyer, Ralf Schlüter, Hermann Ney
**ArXiv:** [1911.08876v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08876v1)
**Published:** 2019-11-20

**Contribution:** The paper demonstrates that applying SpecAugment—time and frequency masking on mel‑spectrograms—to end‑to‑end speech translation models effectively reduces overfitting and yields measurable BLEU gains across multiple language pairs.

**Method:** Implement SpecAugment as a data augmentation step during training of an encoder–decoder ST model; evaluate performance on standard benchmarks while varying data size to assess robustness.

**Key Findings:**
- SpecAugment improves BLEU by up to +2.2% on LibriSpeech En→Fr and +1.2% on IWSLT En→De.
- The augmentation benefits persist across different data regimes, showing consistent gains regardless of training set size.
